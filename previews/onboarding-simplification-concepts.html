Onboarding, rebuilt around one question

Two doors on the homepage, then "Who's sending the package?" — which replaces the path picker, covers the plain "I need a label" case the product already supports but never names, and leaves the link-vs-label fork to a field instead of a screen.

What the code already does

three findings; all three make this smaller than it looks

"I'm shipping something to someone" already works — it's just unnamed. The full-label flow is role-agnostic: step 1 asks "Where should the package be delivered?" (RecipientStepAddress.tsx:142) and never claims the address is yours; step 10 collects the origin. Put your address in the origin and theirs in the destination and you have a plain outbound label — same link_type, same rate lookup, same purchase, same PDF. The decided proposal 2026-06-27_label-confirmation-email-by-role models exactly this: "Full Prepaid Label — the signed-in user (sender = payer) … the destination person." "On 2026-06-27 John created a Full Prepaid Label himself (he picked the destination, the package, and paid — no shared link involved)." The email layer has already been taught this case, down to a dedupe rule for shipping to yourself (OQ4). Only the picker's copy denies it exists.

The only remaining fork is the address. The picker sells two unknowns — their address and the item's weight or size — but MagicGuestimator.tsx already turns "a hardcover cookbook, no rush" into dimensions and weight, and it's wired into the shipping step. Size isn't a fork; it's solved.

Both flows already share step 1. destination is step 1 for full-label and flexible alike (stepRouting.ts:49–79); they only diverge at step 10 vs 20. The fork moves from a screen of its own into the step where the user finds out whether they can answer it.

So the generic case needs no new path, no new enum value, and no migration — which is why "who's sending it?" is a better first question than "which product do you want?": it's the one input that actually changes what happens next.

1 — Two doors

split on who pays, since that's the one thing the flows can't share

Why who-pays is the right split. Everything else — sending, receiving, having the address or not — is answerable inside one flow. Who pays is not: the seller link takes payment on-session from an anonymous buyer (Stripe Payment Element, no account), while the other three charge the account holder. Different money path, different route, so it earns its own door. And it gets one: today /sell has no entry point except the gated third card of the picker.

Why A: both doors stay at the same altitude — each names a situation the user is in. B's second door names the artifact ("a checkout shipping link"), so the reader has to translate "I sold a couch" into a product noun. Two doors at different altitudes read slower than two at the same one. On voice: "I'll pay" puts the user's words in a label the product is speaking, while the title beside it ("Send or receive") is addressed to the user — one voice per label reads cleaner, so A uses "you" throughout.

Why B might still win, and it's not a small point. "Sell an item" is narrower than the product. A seller link with max_shipments = NULL is reusable, and funder is a documented future seam for "seller covers shipping" (migration 040). Anyone who wants the other person to pay at checkout can use it — a shop, a club collecting postage, a repair service. "Create a checkout shipping link" is honest about that range; "Sell an item" trades range for recognizability. A hedges by carrying the range in helper text, but if the non-selling uses matter for launch, B is the more truthful door. This is a positioning call, not a copy call — flagged as OQ1 rather than decided here.

Who-pays is stated once, in the subtitle — "Either way, you're the one paying for shipping" — not repeated on both cards. 5 — Naming

"Flexible" describes an internal capability, not anything a user wants

The real distinction is link vs label. One is a promise filled in later; the other a finished artifact. "Prepaid" does no work on the link (both are prepaid by you) but earns its place on the label, where it tells the sender they owe nothing. Under this flow the names stop being a choice anyone has to understand up front and become a label on something already made — they show up in the dashboard, emails and tracking page, where plain is all they need to be.

Internal link_type Today Set A Recommended Set B — role-led
flexible Flexible Prepaid Shipping Link Shipping link Ship-to-me link
full_label Completed Prepaid Label Prepaid label Ready-to-print label
seller_link Sell & Ship Seller link Buyer-pays link

Rename the strings, not the enum. link_type is a real column with a CHECK (link_type IN ('full_label','flexible')) constraint referenced across ~8 migrations and several RPCs (001_initial_schema.sql:53 onward). Renaming identifiers means a migration plus RPC rewrites for zero user benefit.

What this costs, and what needs deciding first

ordered by how much they'd hurt to get wrong
  • Positioning moves, and that's a product call. SPEC.md:12–34 is recipient-only: "Recipients create a link once, share it with anyone who needs to send them something." Naming the generic case means the one-liner, the value-prop list and the target-user section all change. Worth noting the trade: plain outbound labels are a crowded free market (Pirate Ship, Click-N-Ship) where you'd compete on rates rather than on the coordination problem that makes SendMo distinctive. The build cost is near zero since it already ships — so the real question is whether you want that traffic, and right now it's being decided by omission.
  • pathSlug gets decided mid-flow, not at entry. The URL is /onboarding/:pathSlug/:stepSlug and the path is fixed before step 1 today. Shared steps need a slug before the fork resolves — either a neutral slug that rewrites on the fork, or the path moves out of the URL into flow state. Touches stepRouting.ts, the canAccessStep guards, and Sentry's parameterized route names. This is the load-bearing one.
  • The origin prefill needs wiring. addresses stores verified per-user addresses, but the "Shipping from" row assumes we can resolve a default for the signed-in user — and anonymous first-timers have none, so the row must degrade to the full form without looking broken.
  • "Step N of 5" has to survive the fork. Both branches land on five steps in the map above; worth keeping that true rather than finding out later that it isn't.
  • The escape is reversible. "Actually, I do have their address" restores the typed address, so origin fields persist across the toggle rather than unmounting.
  • RecipientStepPathChoice.tsx is deleted, not refactored — along with every Tier 1 and Tier 2 fix mocked in onboarding-path-choice-tier2.html. That earlier mock is now historical: it documents the measured defects of a screen this proposal removes.
  • Still open — does full_label stay one product or two? A shipping link with everything prefilled is a prepaid label. If the flexible flow accepted a prefilled origin, the two collapse into one link_type. Probably the right eventual shape; nothing above requires deciding it yet.
